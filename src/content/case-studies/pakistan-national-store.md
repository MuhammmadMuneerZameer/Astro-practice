## Store Design & Brand Architecture

PNS sells pieces that compete with gallery sculpture — $7,500 Royal Arabian Stallions,
$8,500 Royal Cheetah Companions, bronze eagles finished in antique gold. At these price
points, the store itself is part of the product. We designed a Shopify experience built
around the brand's core promise: museum-quality craftsmanship, delivered to your home.

The collection architecture was mapped to reflect the brand's range — **Royal Collection**
for flagship prestige pieces, **Ahd-e-Qadeem** for heritage-inspired work, **Mount King**
for animal sculptures, and **New Arrivals** to maintain recency signals and give repeat
visitors a reason to return.

## Luxury UX for High-Ticket Items

Standard ecommerce UX is engineered for impulse — speed, brevity, friction removal.
Luxury ecommerce has different requirements. Buyers spending $7,500–$17,000 need time,
detail, and confidence. We structured every product page around three conversion levers:

- **Material specificity** — antique brass, antique copper, antique silver, antique gold
  finishes described with precision, giving buyers a vocabulary to feel informed
- **Scale and placement** — photography and copy help buyers visualise the piece in their
  own space, critical when the item cannot be seen in a showroom first
- **Policy clarity** — the 7-day exchange policy surfaced early to reduce purchase anxiety
  at this price tier

## International E-commerce Infrastructure

PNS's core ambition is the US market with international expansion beyond it.
We built the checkout, shipping logic, and currency handling to serve this from day one —
the store launched globally rather than building domestic-first and retrofitting later.
Ahmed & Sons Group LLC operates out of St. Petersburg, Florida, which anchors the brand
in the US market while the product's Pakistani artisan origin becomes a differentiator
rather than a barrier.

## Collection Strategy & Merchandising

Across four collections, we structured product naming and merchandising hierarchy to
communicate prestige without alienating first-time visitors. The Royal Collection leads —
positioning the highest-value pieces as the brand's signature. Ahd-e-Qadeem grounds the
brand in heritage and craftsmanship storytelling. New Arrivals gives the catalog motion,
encouraging repeat visits in an otherwise static luxury product category.

## Paid Media — Meta Prospecting & Retargeting

With the store live and the brand infrastructure in place, the focus shifted to traffic.
The challenge in paid media for luxury goods is the same as on the PDP: buyers at this
price point do not convert in a single session. The media strategy was built around that
reality — not chasing last-click attribution, but building a qualified audience that
returns.

We launched Meta campaigns across four structures in parallel:

- **Prospecting (broad and interest-based)** — testing CPMs and creative formats against
  cold audiences to identify which message architecture earns the click at this price tier
- **New customer campaigns** — dedicated ad sets for first-time buyers, separated from
  retargeting to allow clean signal isolation
- **CBO (Campaign Budget Optimisation)** — automated budget allocation across performing
  ad sets to shift spend toward the lowest cost-per-purchase in real time
- **Retargeting** — warm audiences who had visited product pages but not converted,
  sequenced to apply purchase pressure without discounting the product

Across 38 campaigns tested in the first active period, the account achieved an average
outbound CTR of 2.63% and an average CPM of approximately $3.50 — efficient by any
benchmark for cold prospecting on luxury goods. The best-performing campaign reached a
4.40 website purchase rate and a cost per purchase of Rs3,083 (~$11 USD). At a product
price floor of $5,500, that unit economics profile is exceptional.

The immediate priority for the next phase is calibrating Meta's purchase value tracking
so ROAS figures reflect actual AOV and budget-scaling decisions have an accurate signal.
